About

I am an undergraduate in the Department of Automation at Tsinghua University. My research covers embodied AI and robot learning, multi-robot coordination and communication, reinforcement learning, and diffusion models.

I am a research intern in Prof. Wei-Chiu Ma's group at Cornell University, where I study when two humanoid robots should share perception and what they should exchange. I also work with Prof. Ge Liu at UIUC on diffusion models that generate protein sequences and structures together.

I plan to apply to PhD programs for Fall 2028 admission.

Research experience

  • Cornell UniversityResearch Intern, Dept. of Computer Science
    Advisor: Prof. Wei-Chiu Ma
    Jun 2026 – Present
    • Developing real-time perception sharing between two humanoid robots, so that a robot walking backward can climb stairs it cannot see using its partner's view.
    • Showed that robots can usually infer hidden terrain from gait and contact alone, and identified the settings where the partner's view is necessary. In those settings it raises success rates by 20–40 percentage points.
    • Built decentralized policies that combine onboard sensing with messages from the partner, stop safely when the link drops, and resume when it returns. Demonstrated two simulated humanoids climbing stairs together.

    In preparationHumanoid2Humanoid: Shared Sensing for Cooperative Loco-Manipulation. Target: RSS 2027.

  • University of Illinois Urbana-ChampaignResearch Intern, Dept. of Computer Science
    Advisor: Prof. Ge Liu
    Mar 2026 – Present
    • Studying protein sequence–structure co-design in which the sequence is generated by continuous diffusion over learned amino-acid embeddings, built on MultiFlow with its structure module unchanged.
    • Showed that the continuous sequence channel is not limited by its representation, while its derived likelihood term must be tempered for proteins. Traced this to the small vocabulary: 20 embeddings can form a regular simplex, which overstates per-position evidence.
    • Located the remaining gap. Co-designed backbones now match the masked-discrete baseline, and the deficit lies in writing sequences for the model’s own backbones. Built a test that predicts whether a gain in accuracy will turn into more designable structures.

Open source

  • PlugRLwith Chenhao Lu and Zilin Kang
    2025 – Present
    • Co-developed a wire protocol between RL trainers and environments, so that environments on other machines, in other languages, or outside the training cluster can still be trained. Wrote the protocol specification and its conformance checkers.
    • Showed that most silent faults at the environment–trainer boundary pass learning-curve checks and clean evaluation undetected. A step ledger kept on both sides detected and located all 585 faulty runs tested, at a median cost of 3% of training time on Pendulum (CoRL 2026 workshop paper).
    • Released the step ledger as step-ledger, a package for Gymnasium and Stable-Baselines3.
